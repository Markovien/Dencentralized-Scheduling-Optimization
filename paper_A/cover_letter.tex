\begin{letter}{The Editor-in-Chief\\\textit{Engineering Applications of Artificial Intelligence}\\Elsevier}

\opening{Dear Editor,}

We submit for your consideration the manuscript \textit{``Cooperative and non-cooperative
game-theoretic scheduling of production machines and battery-constrained autonomous vehicles
under time-of-use tariffs''} as an original research article.

\textbf{What the paper does.} It develops a decentralised mechanism for scheduling a job shop
in which every operation is preceded by a transport leg carried out by a finite-battery
autonomous vehicle, electricity is priced by a time-of-use tariff, the charging station has
finite capacity, and the planning horizon is a hard deadline. Machines and vehicles decide
for themselves, at events rather than on a time grid. We prove that each stage game is an
exact potential game whose price of stability is one, and then --- this is the paper's centre
of gravity --- we analyse the \emph{cooperative} question that potential games leave open:
whether the agents would stay in the arrangement if they could renegotiate, and how the
savings should be divided.

\textbf{What is new, and why it is not the expected answer.} The natural hypothesis is that
coordination has diminishing returns, so the coalition cost is submodular, the savings game
convex, and the core non-empty with the Shapley value inside it. We show this is false for
this problem. A scarce shared resource that saturates makes the coalition cost
\emph{supermodular}; we exhibit a two-vehicle instance whose core is provably empty, and we
find the same obstruction on essentially every instance of the classical benchmark. Stability
must therefore be \emph{certified} rather than asserted, and we certify it per instance
through the least-core radius, computed exactly. Reporting a certified radius is a weaker
claim than a core-existence theorem, and a true one. Around that result we contribute
decentralised coalition-structure generation that recovers almost all of the
grand-coalition value through pairwise agreements, and a bargaining derivation of the
makespan--energy weight that scalarised bi-objective scheduling normally leaves to the
author.

\textbf{Fit with EAAI.} The contribution is a mechanism with proofs applied to an engineering
system: equilibrium analysis, coalitional stability and mechanism design, evaluated on a
standard manufacturing benchmark. That is EAAI's centre rather than its periphery, and the
formal content is the contribution rather than an ornament on an empirical study.

\textbf{Relation to our own prior work.} We have previously applied potential games with
marginal-contribution utilities to job-shop scheduling with transport (Sanogo et al.,
\textit{Computers \& Industrial Engineering}, 2025; \textit{Journal of Manufacturing
Systems}, 2025). We do not claim the non-cooperative mechanism as new and we say so
explicitly in the introduction. What is new here is: time-of-use tariffs and battery and
charging decisions as first-class decision variables; the entire cooperative layer, which has
no counterpart in that work; a corrected equilibrium theory, in which the potential property
is proved at the scope at which it actually holds; and an exact constraint-programming
reference for the combined problem. No text is reused.

\textbf{Reproducibility.} Every numerical value in the manuscript resolves to a key in a
machine-readable registry produced by a recorded run; no numeral in a results sentence is
typed by hand. The released artefact contains the simulator, all solution concepts, the
benchmark with regenerated tariff profiles, and a test for each formal result that fails if
the result is false --- including a regression test pinning the counterexample whose core
must come out empty. We also report, in the paper itself, what we did \emph{not} establish:
the exact model does not close the full problem on most instances, so no empirical price of
anarchy is reported, and one proposition is stated conditionally because a proof step remains
open.

The manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and all authors have
approved this submission. We declare no competing interests.
